# Tone of Voice

## **Tone of voice**

> Hoe communiceren we?

Even though we now have an in-house design team, that'll provide UX-text along with designs and prototypes, we can’t write every single word. That’s why it’s important for everyone at Embrace to have some basic guidelines for writing UX-text.

The goal here is to make sure there's a cohesive voice throughout. Words, animations, buttons, interaction patterns all matter when creating the best possible experience for our users.

While we have the same voice all the time, our tone changes.

Different situations require different tones — we don’t write error messages the same way we write onboarding content.

### 1. Show expertise and experience, don't boast

We have proven expertise, so our customers know they can trust us with some of their most important processes. We like to share this expertise and experience because we believe it will help customers getting the most out of our software.

We share expertise and experience by adding useful tips, like:

"Write your FAQ in the first person: Use ‘I’s' when framing the question"

### 2. Focused, not limited

We want to make the most important things easy, and everything else possible. That might mean leaving information out sometimes, but it should always be available when needed.

### 3. Be enthusiastic, don't exaggerate

We want to be approachable, but professional. Friendly and sometimes playful. Small moments of delight are okay (even encouraged), but focus on helping users get their jobs done first. Avoid hyperbole and exclamation marks.

So “Great” is okay, but “awesome!!” or “wow, that's cool!” isn’t.

### 4. Friendly and playful, not hilarious all the time

Some of the work our customers use our software for can be monotonous or even boring. So while humour (like small easter eggs) can help, don't overdo it.

The following example shows four variations of the same message:

\_1. “We apologize, but we are experiencing a problem.”

1. “We’re sorry, but we’re experiencing a problem on our end.”
2. “Oops! We’re sorry, but we’re experiencing a problem on our end.”
3. “What did you do!? You broke it! (Just kidding. We’re experiencing a problem on our end.)”\_

* Never use 1
* We generally aim for 3.
* In case of serious errors, where work is lost we use 2
* Only when the impact of the message is fairly low 4 can be used.
